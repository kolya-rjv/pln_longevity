LinAge2
Query Battery

52 queries against a patient someone types into the My Patient tab — each with the query form it should translate to, the behaviour that counts as a pass, and what the system actually returned on 2026-10-04.

What it is for. A repeatable acceptance pass over the LinAge2 path: plain text in, a patient read back and built for one browser session, the clinical clock scored in-process, questions about that patient translated, routed to the right space and answered. Part A tests the tab; Parts B–D the inference over the clock; Part E questions that cross into the rest of the knowledge base; Part F honesty.

What it is not. Not a benchmark with a score, and not a clinical tool. The interesting signal is where years are reported without a cause, where an empty answer is correct, and where a refusal protects a number from a wrong unit.

Verified, not asserted. 47 of the 52 entries were executed by scripts/linage2_battery.py — every query form through POST /query with only the LLM translator stubbed (system prompt, patient block, routing, validation, worker-process execution, warnings all as served), every tab entry through the tab's own handlers — and 47 passed. The other 5 have no query form (∅): their correct answer is a direct reply, which needs the LLM translator and is graded live. Translation itself (question → form) is likewise graded live.

Reading an entry

Each entry carries the question as a person would type it, the form the translator should emit (∅ means a direct answer and nothing executed), the pass criterion, and OBSERVED: what the system returned when the form was run against the named patient.

The distinction the battery is built around

Years are not causes. LinAge2 gives every lab a number of years, but its per-lab weight is a sex-specific projection whose sign is not the lab's clinical direction (CRP is +4.4 months per SD in the female model and −0.2 in the male one). So the knowledge base credits a cause only when the person's own value witnesses it — their HbA1c is elevated, they said they smoke. Everything else is reported as years and left unexplained.

Three shapes follow from that and recur below: a lab that adds years with no cause (albumin); a lab that is high and still takes years off (glucose in the male model); and a lever that returns zero because there is nothing witnessed for it to act on.

Runtime conditions that change how a result reads

The patient lives in one browser session. It is a plain dict in the UI's session state; every question rebuilds its atoms into that one query's space. Nothing is written to the knowledge base.

LinAge2 runs in its own space. Its atoms never enter the shared space (they crash it); a question that needs both is split, and the answers are joined in the order asked.

An empty result is frequently the pass. No absolute risk without a baseline, no lever without a route, no LinAge2 for the built-in patients.
