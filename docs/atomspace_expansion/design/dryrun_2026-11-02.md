## Stage 1: query to papers, papers to background

Query 1 of the slate; drafted prompts S1a and S1b; 39 known PMIDs from the root `.metta` files. A deterministic
resolver checks every recommendation against PubMed: **resolved** = title ratio >= 0.85 and the same first author or
year; **near** = the same first author and year, ratio >= 0.55 (a real paper, title reworded); otherwise **not found**
(the loosest search then only finds same-surname papers on other topics, the signature of an invented reference).

- Recall by a small model invents most references (gpt-5.4-mini: 8 of 14 not found, and "near" titles are rewordings
  of real papers). The current mid and flagship models invented none here. One sol background item named the wrong
  first author of a real paper (Hosokawa for Jung CH 2009; two companion papers); the resolver kept it on title and
  year.
- The prompt says "up to 10" and "a paper you cannot name precisely is worse than no paper"; every model returned
  fewer than 10 (5, 7, 9). Ben's ~600-paper seed assumes 10 per query; at sol medium this query gives 7 + about 7

- Astra was more complete (it alone named Horvath 2019, rapamycin slowing epigenetic ageing of keratinocytes, the
  most direct hit) for 12 times the cost. At the stage-1 scale (about 10 S1a and 100 S1b calls) that is about $4 on
  sol medium against about $46 on astra high.
- Effort `high` on sol exhausted a 10,000-token output limit before any answer: an output limit must leave room for
  reasoning, and a truncated answer must never be cached or retried blind.

## Stage 2: trace and gap call (gpt-6.1-sol, high)

`!(explain &self Rapamycin DNAmGDF15)` on today's runtime stack (25 files, default runtime): status `empty`, 0
results, 0.1 s. The S2 call ($0.020, 2,057 prompt tokens, 1,024 reasoning) returned three `missing_edge` gaps that
follow the query's chain: mTORC1 inhibition to Mitophagy, Mitophagy to Mitochondrial function, Mitochondrial function
to DNAmGDF15, each with `Effect`, a study design and four PubMed terms. Findings:

- An empty trace carries no chain, no partial path and no reason, so the gaps come from the question, not from the
  KB; the trace format (§4 item 5) must say how far the search got and which nodes exist.
- The gaps name nodes in free text ("mTORC1 inhibition", "Mitochondrial function") although the schema card is in
  the prompt; the gap must be bound to KB symbols (or flagged missing_node) before it can be checked as closed.
- The four terms ANDed give 1, 8 and 0 PubMed hits, so "10 papers per gap" is not reachable from search as written.
  The last gap (rapamycin, mitochondrial function, DNAmGDF15, GrimAge) has no paper at all, which is also what S1a's
  model said ("could not name a primary human study"); the loop needs a stop for gaps the literature cannot close.

## The parser, three arms on the same 22 sentences

Driver: the parser's `nl2pln` unmodified, called as `experiments/pubmed/batch_parse_pubmed.py` does (one call per
sentence, `runs=1`, `max_back_forth=10`, the last 5 parses of the same abstract as context), with `to_openrouter`
and `get_embedding` swapped in-process for OpenAI SDK calls and `chaining` stubbed. Deviations: output limit 16k to
32k instead of 128k, strict schema given `additionalProperties: false`, empty `tools` omitted, 2 transient retries
instead of 5. Note: Man Hin's own PubMed script sets `model = "gpt-5.4"`, `effort = "none"`
(`experiments/pubmed/batch_parse_pubmed.py:9-10`), not `nl2pln`'s default of gpt-5.4-mini at `high` that W2 costed.
Sentences: Lu 2019 (7), Johnson 2013 (6), Harrison 2009 (9), split deterministically.

- **The correction loop is mostly the parser disagreeing with itself.** "stmt format" corrections are numbers: the
  prompt asks for `(PointMass 2e-75)`-style values, `stmt_format_check` accepts only `(STV s c)`
  (`checker_functions.py:28-40`; W1 finding 10), so the model is sent back until it drops or rewrites the number.
  That is where the numbers go: p-values were lost in every arm; Harrison's "14% for females and 9% for males" survived
  only on terra.
- **The LLM truth values carry almost no information**: on terra 186 of 209 statements are `(STV 1.0 0.9)`,
  `(STV 1.0 0.95)` or `(STV 1.0 1.0)`; luna and sol likewise. Keeping them out of Layer B costs nothing.
- **Every parse is anchored to the time of parsing**, not the paper: the prompt's "Current time" context becomes
  `(TemporalBefore evt 2026-11-02T18:24:13+04:00)` in past-tense sentences.
- **Open vocabulary**: 110 to 154 distinct predicates for 22 sentences, dominated by `IsA`, `Theme`, `Agent`,
  `HasAttribute`; the implication shape matches neither the restricted library nor `pln_chainer` (W1). Layer A as
  parsed is a store of readable event graphs, not something today's chainers consume.
- Luna is cheapest but thin (2.8 statements per sentence, the most structural corrections, the slowest); sol at low is
  the fastest but the fewest first-try passes; terra at medium parses most fully, keeps the most numbers, costs 20%
  less than sol. **Terra at medium is the size that suits the parser.**

### One full text: Johnson 2013, 55 body sentences, luna at high

To check the projection on full-text sentences (Results are denser with numbers than abstracts): 55 of 55 parsed,
$0.170 in all, **$0.0031 per sentence** (the same paper's abstract cost $0.0030 per sentence on luna), 1.42 chat
calls per sentence, 38 first-try passes, corrections stmt format 16, undefined predicate 4, unused predicate 3;
3.0 statements per sentence; 200 distinct predicates for one paper; 74 s per sentence, 68 minutes for the paper.
**Cost per sentence holds on full text, so the projections above stand. Numbers do not:** 39 of 55 sentences carry
numbers and 23 of their 71 numbers survive (32%). Full text is where the effect sizes are, and the parser's own
format check removes most of them; that makes the PointMass/STV fix the first thing to settle with Man Hin before
any full-text Layer A run.
